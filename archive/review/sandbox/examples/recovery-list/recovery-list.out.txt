§3.4 — a voucher reveals only itself

  on the wire  {"key":"lSsNjsT3evpDW6UbuftsCqsxJ4eTe8pS21eX5p9QTNQ","salt":"saltsis","sig":"ttyqfT-I4auqFG0udf45r76o5gavmZEnStB0E5oAcQAKIAYNpkJRz9LjIqJfu8ZiolEB9Gtabq9w-RYtVOIHDw"}

  A leaf is the hash of a salt AND a key, so testing a candidate key needs that
  member's salt. The published link carries sis's salt and no other:

  salted leaves, saltsis revealed   identifies sis
  salted leaves, no salt revealed   identifies nobody
  if a leaf were SHA-256(key)       identifies mum, sis, bro

  Three family keys is a guessable space, and a bare hash of the key falls to that
  scan in a second. The salt is what keeps the rest of the list unreadable.

§3.4 — the count of leaves is public, and MUST be

  leaves anyone can count   3
  vouchers that count       1  (sis)
  more than half of 3       2

  A contest is settled by a majority of the recovery list at the split (§3.6). Hiding
  the count would leave that majority with no denominator, and a forger could call his
  one voucher a majority of a list only he can count. See examples/contest/.

§3.4 — `k` is the threshold the author set for a restore to be valid

  k                       2 of 3
  sis alone               1 counted — below k, so the link is not valid
  mum and sis             2 counted — the link is valid
  mum's key, sis's salt   0 counted — a leaf binds the salt and the key together

  a reader over the whole profile: ok, chain ending on 17Ffa8rSZgnuFbV_5lfpNbt29t3qbWSOZgD2Qzfwy2M

  A *contested* identity is settled not by `k` but by a majority of the list (§3.6).

§3.4 — the list MAY be empty

  committed                 {"k":1,"leaves":[]}
  mum and sis vouch anyway  0 counted — no leaf can match an empty list
  the chain walks?          false

  The cost is exact: no restore is possible, and a lost key is a lost identity. Where
  the list is not empty, a member can be a person, a backup key you keep yourself, or your
  host — a leaf does not say which, and nothing outside your app knows.
