§6 — three visibilities, one mechanism

  public      post 1                in the clear: n, at, text
  encrypted   post 6                in the clear: n, at, encrypted   an audience of four
  a DM        post 5                in the clear: n, at, encrypted   an audience of two — mum, and alice herself

  A DM is that set with one member besides the author. All three are ordinary signed posts
  (§2) — body, one \n, 86 signature characters — and the index lists all three alike (§4).

§6.1 — one ephemeral per message, one slot per recipient, the content once

  epk                               bulurRC1e4YYuDGwVZj_Yh9ZgswZoponWSc5JsAp5z8
  content key                       ignPR4B2H28CyC3JWdlMWpBKY9enPyRVYs4O9CatkIw   32 random bytes, used once
  carrier                           pukq6VMQM9Sbp4ae71bJjcKbLLpFuWi47cDS60xH7CY:5

  mum's slot, from her reading key R and this message's ephemeral. Wherever epk appears in a
  derivation it is the 32 raw bytes; the carrier is ASCII text:
    tag(8) || kek(32) || knonce(12) = HKDF-SHA256(ikm = Z, salt = epk, info = "openfeed/v1/slot", 52)
    Z = X25519(eph priv, R)         7d8c28c57811b9f2df3f8dd04859ffd8…
    tag(8)                          SzNzzQy4o2c
    kek(32)                         220656a717782418daaa9df39a08bcd7…
    knonce(12)                      138c98fae0d6ea453c41bd3e
    wrapped = ChaCha20-Poly1305(kek, knonce, content key, aad = epk)
      2rsCQZAjQMhlxocGQd4baI0tsCQiZqRX8BtHmJ8mihXiGd5DtWA0mmPvzLY0Ite-

  and the content, once:
    plain                           345 bytes of UTF-8 JSON — the audience, then the content members
    padded                          2-byte big-endian length, plain, zeros to 512 (§6.4 — examples/padding/)
    ct = ChaCha20-Poly1305(content key, 12 zero bytes, padded, aad = epk || carrier)
      F0a_r7lGklcYcRiWYCqFFFNp-LVbDvXTILfYICimJP1…   704 characters

  The all-zero nonce is safe for the reason it is safe in age's key wrap: the content key is 32
  random bytes and MUST NOT be reused across messages, so that (key, nonce) pair is used exactly
  once. The same holds one level up — knonce is derived from the ephemeral, so an ephemeral reused
  across two posts wraps two content keys under one (kek, knonce). One ephemeral per message.

§6.2 — the carrier is associated data, so a lifted envelope does not open

  the thief's post 1 verifies       true   under his own key — it really is his post
  mum opens it at his carrier       null   (jOg0rruvMf…:1)
  a client that passes no carrier   null
  at the post it was published in   "I am leaving him on Friday"

  There is no "forgot to compare": the carrier is not a field checked afterwards, it is what
  the unwrap is keyed against. Had the associated data been epk alone, the unwrap would take no
  carrier at all, and the same bytes would open wherever they were pasted:
    "I am leaving him on Friday" — her words, under his name.
  (That counterfactual reuses the content key and nonce over the identical plaintext on purpose;
  only the associated data differs, and nothing leaks. It is not a thing a publisher may do.)

§6.3 — a tag is a hint, never a decision

  scanning for mum's own tag        finds it (slot 2 of 8 here; §6.4 says nothing about placement), and unwraps once
  ignoring every tag, trying all    2 unwraps here, up to 8 — conformant, merely slower
  the same plaintext either way     true

  and a slot carrying mum's own tag whose unwrap fails, placed first:
    she keeps scanning and opens    "I am leaving him on Friday"

  A reader that stopped at the first match would decide a message was not for it on eight
  bytes it does not control. The tag saves work; it never answers the question.

§6.3 — tags are blinded per message

  mum's tag on post 5               SzNzzQy4o2c
  mum's tag on post 6               lYaMx_t160M
  slot tags the two posts share     0 of 8

  A tag is derived through the message's own ephemeral, so an observer holding every published
  reading key derives none of them, and one recipient's slots do not link across posts.

§6.5 — the audience is inside, and each entry names a person

  mum opens post 6 and finds 4 entries — alice's own among them, or alice could not read her own outbox:
    key pukq6VMQM9…                 read cLoW-OhUZj…   loc https://alice.example/alice
    key 5ywjllCxE-…                 read Yu9nDDrlZO…   loc https://mom.example/mom
    key lSsNjsT3ev…                 read kCqfgIlkM7…   loc https://sis.example/sis
    key Tt-buDzctW…                 read zo8EeoQJxa…   loc https://bro.example/bro

  Mum replies, and a comment on an encrypted post is encrypted in turn (§6). For each entry she
  reads the profile at `loc`, refuses it unless its `anchor` is that entry's `key` (§3.1), and
  encrypts to the `read` key that profile carries (§3.8):
    alice true   mum true   sis true   bro true
    and a profile served at bro's loc under someone else's anchor is refused: null

  Had the audience been reading keys and nothing else, mum would hold an X25519 key for sis and
  for bro and nothing that leads to a profile, so she could address only the member she already
  knew. Her reply goes out to two, and nothing anywhere raises an error:
    alice true   mum true   sis false   bro false
  The thread splits in half and neither half is told.

§6.6 — on an encrypted post, rel, target and media go inside

  mum's post 13, in the clear       {"n":13,"at":"2026-08-19T19:00:00Z","encrypted"…
  "reply" in the public bytes?      false   it is inside: rel="reply"
  alice's key in the public bytes?  false   the target is inside, all four members of it

  A public post keeps rel and target in the clear, so public threading, relocation riding along
  in a reply (§3.7) and the rumor rule (§7.5) are unaffected for anything a stranger could see
  anyway — and unavailable for anything encrypted. examples/moving/ and examples/top-and-rumors/.

§7.4 — a reader outside the audience verifies it completely and hands it back opaque

  post 5 is Appendix B.8 byte for byte, at address 52zvhtC1WqYWvwKJqqqfxkzXBNSyrGMHFCGNLBEhhcM, and the host's own
  reading key opens null. Opening it is the client's business; the read is examples/the-reader/.

  What the host learns is that an encrypted post exists, when, and roughly how big: post 5 is
  1574 bytes and post 6, to four people, is 2257. The audience is inside, so a large one shows in the
  bucket the body lands in; §6.4's floor hides the small end.

Every line above is asserted.
