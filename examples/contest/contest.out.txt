§3.6 — a recovery list is kept per chain length, and is never overwritten

§3.6 — a majority of the list at the split wins, on exactly one side

  her link at the split          hers his  his link at the split               verdict
  a restore, mum and sis         2    0    a rotation with the key he holds    host — his branch rejected
  a rotation she signed herself  0    0    a rotation with the key he holds    identity — contested
  a rotation she signed herself  0    1    a restore he vouched for himself    identity — his link does not hold
  a restore, mum and sis         2    1    a restore he vouched for himself    identity — his link does not hold
  a restore, mum and sis         2    2    a restore mum was made to sign too  identity — contested

  A `sig` is not a vote: row 2 is two signed rotations, and each proves only that whoever
  held A2 moved. He held it too. Rows 3 and 4 never reach the contest: one voucher of three
  is not a valid link (§3.3). Row 5 is a majority on both sides — mum vouched for him.

§3.6 — he moves first: the key he holds is her live key, and he rotates it to his own

  a reader pinned at version 2, whose chain ends on A2   he rotates A2 → his key:  ok — no split, and it follows him
  her restore, mum and sis, reaches it at version 4       ok — a split at 2, hers has the majority, it follows her: A3
  On his hub her restore may never arrive. Withholding is the move no rule here answers (§13.3).

§3.3 / §3.6 — one bar, a majority: the case the protocol exists for

  the list                  3 leaves — mum, sis, and the ex. any two of them can bring her back
  her link                  a rotation she signed herself      0 vouchers
  his link                  a restore he vouched for himself   1 voucher, his own — not more than half
  at a split                identity: the chain of key changes does not hold
  as an extension           he appends A3 → his key, vouched by himself alone:  identity: the chain of key changes does not hold

  A restore is valid only when more than half of the list vouches, so a listed member alone
  can neither win a split nor quietly extend the chain to a key of his own. A lower threshold
  here would be a second door into the identity that rule 4 never watches, which is why the
  spec has no `k`: the majority is the one bar, for validity and for a contest alike.

§3.3 — the price of the majority rule, and the repair the single link shape buys

  a list of two, 2 leaves — mum and sis; the ex is not on this one
  her link                a restore mum vouched alone   1 of 2 — not more than half
  verdict                 identity: the chain of key changes does not hold
  She is stuck until a second member vouches. That is what the majority rule costs.

§3.6 — two limits a reader cannot escape, and the only exit

  a cold reader meets his branch first  ok — it follows him, adopting 1 leaves at index 2
  alice's real profile then reads       identity: the chain of key changes does not hold
  The real Alice is the one it rejects, and nothing in the protocol repairs that.

  a list change reaches other readers only through a link (§3.5):
  alice adds bro at the same chain length   the pinned reader still holds 3 leaves at length 2

  the exit (§3.1): a person hands the reader the key her chain currently ends on — A3 — and
