§10 — anyone you published to is a backup nobody set up on purpose

  mum's reader kept every byte it was served, and she last looked on the 20th of July.
    she holds  /profile  /index  /posts/1  /posts/2  /posts/3
    handed back and verified under alice's anchor key: 5 of 5

  and the limit, exactly:
    post 4  a message to sis; mum was never in its audience  in her copy: no
    post 5  published after she last looked                  in her copy: no

  A fallback, not a guarantee: it covers what they could see and proves nothing about
  completeness. Only alice's own index says how much there was.

§13.1 — no mechanism takes back what an audience member already read

  post 3 was family-only, and the operator is family: his reading key is in its audience.
    he opens it from bytes he already holds, no host involved:  "the appointment is on Friday"
    the same envelope now that she has left his hub entirely:   still opens
    a key that was never in that audience (mum, on post 4):     null
    sis, who was in it:                                         "I am leaving him on Friday"

  Encryption chose who; it cannot un-choose them, and a withdrawal does not reach a copy. §5.6
  says the same from the other side: a private message is provable by its recipient forever.
  The answer to that operator is exit (§10), not secrecy — and exit is what the copy is.
